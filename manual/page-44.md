# Disposal

From the date on which European Directives 2012/19/EU (ElektroG) and 2013/56/EU (BattG) take effect, the following applies:

Electrical and electronic equipment and batteries must not be disposed of with household waste. At the end of their service life, the consumer is legally obliged to return electrical and electronic equipment to the public collection points set up for this purpose, or to a local specialist workshop or an authorised partner. The details of this are governed by the respective national law. The symbols attached to the products refer to these provisions:

> [Figure: Disposal symbols — a crossed-out wheeled bin (WEEE) labelled "Lithium-Ionen (Li-Ion)", and the CE mark.]

**WEEE Reg. No.: DE60761781**

By reusing, recycling or otherwise recovering old appliances, you make an important contribution to protecting our environment.

In Germany, the disposal regulations stated above apply to batteries and accumulators in accordance with the Battery Ordinance (Batterieverordnung). Batteries are subject to mandatory disposal. They contain toxic heavy metals and must therefore be treated as hazardous waste. Disposal is carried out by a local specialist workshop or an authorised partner.

## Charger

Dispose of the device in accordance with the environmental regulations applicable in your country. Electrical waste must not be disposed of together with household waste. You can obtain further information from your local authority, or from a local specialist workshop or an authorised partner. Once the end of its service life has been reached, render the discarded device unusable by pulling the mains plug out of the socket and cutting through the power cable.

## Other Components

Dispose of the components in accordance with the environmental regulations applicable in your country. Electrical waste must not be disposed of together with household waste. For advice on recycling, contact your local authority or a local specialist workshop or an authorised partner.
